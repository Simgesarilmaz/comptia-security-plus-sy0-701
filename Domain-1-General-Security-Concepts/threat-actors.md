# Threat Actors and Motivations

Understanding threat actors, their motivations, and attack methods is essential for identifying risks and building effective security controls.

---

## 1. Objectives vs. Motivations

### Objective
The specific goal a threat actor wants to achieve during an attack.

Examples:
- Stealing sensitive data
- Disrupting business operations
- Gaining unauthorized access
- Deploying ransomware

### Motivation
The underlying reason that drives a threat actor to perform an attack.

Examples:
- Financial profit
- Political ideology
- Revenge
- Espionage

---

## 2. Common Threat Actor Motivations

### Data Exfiltration
Unauthorized extraction or theft of sensitive data from a system or network.

Examples:
- Customer records
- Intellectual property
- Credentials
- Financial data

---

### Financial Gain
Attacks designed to generate profit.

Examples:
- Ransomware attacks
- Banking trojans
- Credit card theft
- Business email compromise (BEC)

---

### Blackmail and Extortion
Threatening to expose stolen data unless payment or demands are met.

Examples:
- Ransomware groups
- Data leak threats

---

### Service Disruption
Interrupting systems, services, or business operations.

Examples:
- Distributed Denial-of-Service (DDoS)
- Infrastructure sabotage

---

### Hacktivism
Attacks driven by political, ideological, or social beliefs.

Examples:
- Website defacement
- Information leaks
- Public campaigns

---

### Ethical Security Testing
Authorized testing performed to identify vulnerabilities and improve security posture.

Examples:
- Penetration testing
- Red team exercises

---

### Revenge
Attacks motivated by personal grievances or workplace conflicts.

Examples:
- Insider sabotage
- Data deletion
- Credential misuse

---

### Espionage
Stealing confidential information for intelligence or competitive advantage.

Examples:
- Government intelligence gathering
- Corporate espionage

---

### Cyber Warfare
Nation-state cyber operations targeting another country's infrastructure, economy, or security.

Examples:
- Power grid attacks
- Military intelligence operations

---

## 3. Characteristics of Threat Actors

## Insider Threats
Threats originating from individuals inside an organization.

Examples:
- Employees
- Contractors
- Vendors
- Former staff

Risks:
- Data theft
- Sabotage
- Privilege abuse
- Negligence

---

## External Threats
Threats originating outside the organization.

Examples:
- Hackers
- Criminal groups
- Competitors
- Nation-state actors

---

## 4. Levels of Sophistication

### Script Kiddies
Low-skilled attackers who rely on pre-built tools and public exploits.

Characteristics:
- Limited technical knowledge
- Opportunistic attacks

## 5. Threat Vectors vs. Attack Surface

### Threat Vector
The method or path used to perform an attack.

Examples:
- Phishing emails
- Malicious attachments
- Social engineering
- USB devices

---

### Attack Surface
All possible entry points where an attacker may attempt exploitation.

Examples:
- Web applications
- Open ports
- Wireless networks
- User endpoints
- Cloud services

---

## 6. Common Threat Vectors

### Phishing
Fraudulent communication designed to steal credentials or deliver malware.

Types:
- Email phishing
- Smishing (SMS)
- Vishing (Voice)

---

### Malicious Files
Files designed to execute malicious code.

Examples:
- PDFs
- Office documents
- Executables
- Image files with embedded payloads

---

### Removable Media
Malware delivered through physical devices.

Examples:
- USB drives
- External hard drives

---

### Unsecured Networks
Exploitation of insecure communication channels.

Examples:
- Public Wi-Fi
- Misconfigured Bluetooth connections

---

## 7. Defending Against Threat Actors

## Tactics, Techniques, and Procedures (TTPs)

TTPs describe how threat actors operate.

Understanding TTPs helps organizations:
- Detect attacks earlier
- Improve threat intelligence
- Build stronger defenses

---

## Deception Technologies

### Honeypot
A decoy system designed to attract attackers.

### Honeynet
A network of multiple honeypots.

### Honeyfile
A fake file designed to detect unauthorized access.

### Honeytoken
Fake data that triggers alerts when accessed.

---

## Key Security+ Exam Tip

**Objective = What the attacker wants**  
**Motivation = Why the attacker acts**  
**Threat Vector = How the attack happens**  
**Attack Surface = Where the attack happens**
