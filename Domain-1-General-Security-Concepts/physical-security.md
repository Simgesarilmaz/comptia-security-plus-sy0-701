# Physical Security

Physical security focuses on protecting people, facilities, equipment, and other tangible assets from theft, damage, sabotage, or unauthorized access.

In cybersecurity, physical security is critical because even the strongest digital controls can fail if physical access is compromised.

---

## 1. Physical Security Controls

Physical security controls are designed to deter, detect, delay, or prevent unauthorized physical access.

---

## Fencing

Fences establish security boundaries and act as visible deterrents.

### Benefits

- Defines restricted areas
- Prevents unauthorized entry
- Delays intruders
- Increases response time for security personnel

### Security+ Exam Tip

Fences mainly prevent unauthorized access by people.

---

## Bollards

Bollards are strong physical barriers designed to stop vehicles.

### Common Uses

- Building entrances
- Data centers
- Government facilities
- Critical infrastructure

### Protection Against

- Vehicle ramming attacks
- Unauthorized vehicle access

### Security+ Exam Tip

Fences protect against people.  
Bollards protect against vehicles.

---

## 2. Physical Brute Force Attacks

Attackers may attempt physical entry using force.

---

## Forcible Entry

Unauthorized access by physically breaking into a facility.

Examples:

- Breaking doors
- Breaking windows
- Cutting fences

### Countermeasures

- Reinforced doors
- Deadbolt locks
- Metal door frames
- Security glass

---

## Security Device Tampering

Attackers may attempt to disable security controls.

Examples:

- Disconnecting cameras
- Cutting alarm cables
- Disabling sensors

### Countermeasures

- Redundant systems
- Tamper detection sensors
- Backup power systems

---

## Attacks Against Security Personnel

Security guards may be manipulated, distracted, or physically confronted.

### Countermeasures

- Security awareness training
- Conflict resolution training
- Emergency response procedures

---

## Vehicle Ramming Attacks

Vehicles may be used to break through barriers.

### Countermeasures

- Bollards
- Reinforced barriers
- Vehicle access control systems

---

## 3. Surveillance Systems

Surveillance systems help monitor, detect, and record suspicious activity.

---

## Video Surveillance

Security cameras monitor physical environments.

### Common Features

- Motion detection
- Night vision
- Facial recognition
- Pan-Tilt-Zoom (PTZ)

### Common Deployment Areas

- Data centers
- Server rooms
- Entry points
- Telecom closets

---

## Security Guards

Security personnel provide flexible physical security.

### Responsibilities

- Identity verification
- Visitor monitoring
- Incident response
- Emergency assistance

---

## Lighting

Proper lighting improves visibility and deters criminal activity.

### Benefits

- Reduces hiding spots
- Improves camera quality
- Increases detection capability

---

## Sensors

Sensors automatically detect unauthorized movement or environmental changes.

---

## Infrared Sensors

Detect body heat or temperature changes.

---

## Pressure Sensors

Detect weight or movement on floors or restricted surfaces.

---

## Microwave Sensors

Detect movement using microwave signals.

---

## Ultrasonic Sensors

Detect movement using sound wave reflections.

---

## 4. Surveillance Bypass Techniques

Attackers may attempt to bypass surveillance systems.

---

## Visual Obstruction

Blocking camera visibility.

Examples:

- Spray paint
- Tape
- Objects placed over cameras

---

## Blinding

Using bright light to overwhelm cameras.

Examples:

- Flashlights
- Laser pointers

---

## Acoustic Interference

Disrupting microphones using noise or signal interference.

---

## Electromagnetic Interference (EMI)

Disrupting electronic surveillance equipment using electromagnetic signals.

---

## Physical Tampering

Directly damaging or disabling surveillance devices.

Examples:

- Cutting cables
- Disconnecting power
- Destroying sensors

---

## 5. Access Control Mechanisms

Access controls regulate who can enter restricted areas.

---

## Access Control Vestibules (Mantraps)

A mantrap is a controlled entry system with two doors where only one door opens at a time.

### Purpose

Prevent unauthorized physical access.

### Helps Prevent

- Tailgating
- Piggybacking

---

## Piggybacking vs. Tailgating

---

## Piggybacking

An authorized user knowingly allows another person to enter.

Example:

"Can you hold the door for me?"

---

## Tailgating

An unauthorized person follows an authorized user without permission or awareness.

### Security+ Exam Tip

Piggybacking = User is aware  
Tailgating = User is unaware

---

## 6. Door Lock Types

Different locks provide different levels of physical security.

---

## Padlocks

Portable locking devices.

### Risk

Can be vulnerable to cutting or picking.

---

## Pin and Tumbler Locks

Traditional mechanical door locks.

Common in homes and offices.

---

## Numeric Locks

Require a PIN or access code.

---

## Wireless Locks

Use wireless communication for access control.

Examples:

- NFC
- RFID
- Bluetooth

---

## Biometric Locks

Use unique biological characteristics.

Examples:

- Fingerprint
- Retina scan
- Facial recognition

---

## Cipher Locks

Mechanical locks using numeric combinations.

---

## 7. Biometric Security Challenges

Biometric systems may produce authentication errors.

---

## False Acceptance Rate (FAR)

The system incorrectly grants access to an unauthorized person.

---

## False Rejection Rate (FRR)

The system incorrectly denies access to an authorized person.

---

## Crossover Error Rate (CER)

The point where FAR and FRR intersect.

Lower CER generally means better biometric accuracy.

### Security+ Exam Tip

Lower CER = Better biometric performance.

---

## 8. Access Badges

Access badges provide electronic identification.

Common technologies:

- RFID
- NFC
- Smart cards

---

## Badge Cloning

Attackers duplicate access cards to gain unauthorized entry.

### Countermeasures

- Strong encryption
- Multi-factor authentication
- Shielded badge holders
- Access log monitoring

---

## Key Security+ Takeaways

- Physical security protects people, facilities, and equipment.
- Fences deter people; bollards stop vehicles.
- Cameras, guards, lighting, and sensors work together.
- Mantraps help prevent tailgating and piggybacking.
- Biometrics use FAR, FRR, and CER measurements.
- Access badges should be protected against cloning attacks.
