# Security Awareness

Security awareness is the ability of individuals to recognize security threats, understand risks, and respond appropriately to protect organizational assets.

Security awareness helps reduce human-related security vulnerabilities and strengthens overall security posture.

---

## 1. Purpose of Security Awareness

The primary goals of security awareness are:

- Help employees recognize security threats
- Protect confidentiality, integrity, and availability of data
- Reduce human error and insider risks
- Promote secure behavior in daily operations

---

## 2. Focus Areas

Security awareness programs typically focus on:

- Common cyber threats
- Social engineering attacks
- Password security
- Safe digital communication
- Physical security awareness
- Secure remote work practices

---

## 3. Insider Threats

An insider threat originates from individuals within the organization who intentionally or unintentionally create security risks.

### Common Sources

- Current employees
- Former employees
- Contractors
- Vendors
- Business partners

### Common Risks

- Data theft
- Unauthorized access
- Privilege misuse
- Accidental data exposure
- Sabotage

---

## 4. Recognizing Insider Threat Indicators

Behavioral indicators may help identify potential insider threats.

---

## Altered State or Substance Abuse

Individuals under the influence may make poor security decisions.

Possible risks:

- Reduced judgment
- Policy violations
- Increased manipulation risk

---

## Emotional Distress

Emotional instability may increase security risks.

Examples:

- Depression
- Workplace frustration
- Isolation
- Behavioral changes

Possible impact:

- Ignoring security procedures
- Increased susceptibility to social engineering

---

## Lifestyle Inconsistencies

Unusual spending habits that do not match known income.

Possible indicators:

- Financial pressure
- Unauthorized financial gain
- Potential data selling

---

## Financial Difficulties

Financial stress may increase insider threat risk.

Possible outcomes:

- Bribery
- Fraud
- Intellectual property theft

---

## 5. Insider Threat Program

A strong insider threat program should include:

- Security awareness training
- Behavioral reporting procedures
- Employee support programs
- Privacy-respecting monitoring
- Management involvement

---

## 6. Password Security

Strong password practices are critical for account security.

### Goals

- Create strong passwords
- Prevent credential theft
- Reduce unauthorized access

---

## Password Reuse Risks

Using the same password across multiple systems creates major security risks.

### Example

If one website is breached, attackers may attempt credential stuffing against other services.

---

## Password Managers

Password managers securely store and manage credentials.

### Benefits

- Prevent password reuse
- Generate strong passwords
- Encrypted credential storage
- Simplified account management

### Common Features

- Auto-fill
- Encryption
- Secure syncing
- Password generation

---

## 7. Social Engineering Awareness

Social engineering attacks manipulate people instead of technology.

### Key Principle

> People are often the weakest link in security.

---

## Common Social Engineering Threats

- Phishing
- Vishing
- Smishing
- Pretexting
- Impersonation
- Baiting

---

## 8. Situational Awareness

Situational awareness means being conscious of your surroundings and recognizing suspicious behavior.

### Common Threats

---

## Shoulder Surfing

Attackers observe users entering sensitive information.

Examples:

- Passwords
- PIN codes
- Badge access codes

---

## Eavesdropping

Attackers secretly listen to conversations to gather sensitive information.

Examples:

- Phone calls
- Meetings
- Video conferences

---

## Countermeasures

- Privacy screens
- Secure communication practices
- Awareness of surroundings

---

## 9. Piggybacking vs. Tailgating

Both attacks involve unauthorized physical access.

---

## Piggybacking

The authorized user knowingly allows another person to enter.

Example:

"Can you hold the door for me?"

---

## Tailgating

The attacker follows an authorized user without their knowledge.

### Security+ Exam Tip

Piggybacking = User is aware  
Tailgating = User is unaware

---

## 10. Dumpster Diving

Attackers search discarded materials for sensitive information.

Examples:

- Printed documents
- Password notes
- Network diagrams
- Employee records

### Countermeasures

- Document shredding
- Secure disposal policies
- Locked disposal containers

---

## 11. Operational Security (OPSEC)

Operational security protects sensitive business information from being exploited.

### Common Targets

- Daily routines
- Internal procedures
- Business strategies
- Social media posts

### Goal

Prevent attackers from collecting intelligence.

---

## 12. Technological Social Engineering

Attackers may use physical devices to deliver malware.

---

## Baiting Attacks

Attackers leave infected devices for victims to use.

Examples:

- USB drives
- Phone chargers
- External storage devices

### Security Best Practice

> Only use trusted devices, cables, and chargers.

---

## 13. Pressure Tactics

Attackers often create emotional pressure.

Examples:

- Urgency
- Fear
- Authority
- Panic

Goal:

To bypass normal verification procedures.

---

## 14. Security Policies and Handbooks

Security policies define organizational rules and expectations.

### Policy

High-level security rules and principles.

Examples:

- Password policy
- Acceptable use policy
- Remote work policy

---

## Handbook

Detailed guidance documents that explain how to follow policies.

Examples:

- Employee handbook
- Security handbook
- Compliance handbook

### Important Principle

Policies must be understood and applied—not just read.

---

## 15. Data Destruction

Sensitive information must be securely destroyed.

### Common Methods

- Paper shredding
- Secure media destruction
- Digital wiping
- Physical destruction

---

## 16. Remote and Hybrid Work Security

Remote work creates additional security risks.

### Common Risks

- Public Wi-Fi
- Device theft
- Weak home networks
- Unsecured personal devices

### Security Controls

- VPN
- Multi-factor authentication
- Company-managed devices
- BYOD policies
- Encrypted collaboration tools

---

## 17. Building a Security Culture

Technology alone cannot secure an organization.

Security culture creates shared responsibility across all employees.

---

## Security Culture Lifecycle

### 1. Change Management

- Leadership support
- Organizational commitment

### 2. Development

- Policy creation
- Training programs
- Resource allocation

### 3. Execution

- Continuous training
- Security simulations
- Threat updates

### 4. Monitoring and Improvement

- Compliance monitoring
- Feedback collection
- Process improvement

---

## Benefits of Security Culture

- Stronger security posture
- Reduced human error
- Better threat detection
- Improved organizational trust

---

- User education
- Policies
- Awareness training
- Behavioral controls
