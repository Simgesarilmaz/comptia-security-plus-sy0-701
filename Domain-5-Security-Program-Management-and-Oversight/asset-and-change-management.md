# Asset and Change Management

## Asset Management

Asset management is the process of managing, tracking, and protecting organizational assets throughout their lifecycle.

Assets may include:

- Hardware
- Software
- Mobile devices
- Intellectual property
- Business data
- Brand reputation

---

## Primary Objectives

Asset management helps organizations:

- Maximize asset value
- Minimize security and operational risk
- Establish ownership and accountability
- Support centralized management

Organizations often use **Mobile Device Management (MDM)** and similar platforms for centralized control.

---

## Asset Lifecycle

| Phase | Description |
|-------|-------------|
| **Acquisition and Procurement** | Purchasing hardware or software based on business and security requirements |
| **Assignment and Tracking** | Defining ownership, location, and business purpose |
| **Monitoring** | Tracking asset health, compliance, and usage |
| **Disposal and Decommissioning** | Secure removal, sanitization, and retirement |

### Important Note

Every asset should have:

- A unique asset identifier
- A designated owner
- A documented business purpose

---

## Asset Classification

Assets are often categorized by business value and risk.

### Tangible Assets

Physical assets such as:

- Computers
- Servers
- Networking devices
- Industrial equipment

---

### Intangible Assets

Non-physical assets such as:

- Intellectual property
- Brand reputation
- Customer trust
- Business processes

---

## Asset Categorization

| Category | Security Approach |
|----------|-------------------|
| **High-Value Assets** | Stronger security controls and closer monitoring |
| **Low-Value Assets** | Standard controls, possible reuse or recycling |

---

## Asset Tracking Methods

Organizations may track assets using:

- Inventory management systems
- MDM platforms
- Security monitoring tools
- Physical asset enumeration

### MDM Benefits

MDM solutions help organizations:

- Enforce security policies
- Track lost devices
- Perform remote wipe operations
- Manage application deployment

## Data Sanitization Methods

| Method | Description | Device Reusability |
|--------|-------------|--------------------|
| **Overwriting** | Rewrites storage using random data patterns | Reusable |
| **Degaussing** | Uses magnetic fields to destroy data | Not reusable |
| **Secure Erase** | Firmware-based secure deletion | Reusable |
| **Cryptographic Erase (CE)** | Deletes encryption keys, making data inaccessible | Reusable |
| **Physical Destruction** | Shredding, melting, or incineration | Permanent destruction |

---

## Disposal Certification

Organizations should document asset disposal activities for:

- Audit requirements
- Compliance verification
- Chain of accountability

---

## Data Retention

Data should only be retained for:

- Legal requirements
- Business operations
- Regulatory compliance
- Security investigations

### Security Principle

The more data an organization stores, the more data it must protect.

---

## Mobile Asset Deployment Models

Organizations commonly use the following mobile device ownership models.

| Model | Description | Advantages | Risks |
|-------|-------------|------------|-------|
| **BYOD (Bring Your Own Device)** | Employees use personal devices | Lower cost | Reduced security control |
| **COPE (Corporate-Owned, Personally Enabled)** | Company-owned devices with personal use allowed | Stronger security | Higher cost |
| **CYOD (Choose Your Own Device)** | Employees select from approved devices | Balanced flexibility and control | Privacy concerns may exist |

### Selection Factors

Organizations should evaluate:

- Security requirements
- Budget constraints
- User satisfaction
- Privacy considerations

# Change Management

Change management ensures that system changes are implemented in a controlled, planned, and documented manner.

### Primary Goal

Implement change without causing:

- Service disruptions
- Data loss
- Security gaps

---

## Why Change Management Matters

Uncontrolled changes may cause:

- Downtime
- Security vulnerabilities
- Application failures
- Compliance violations

Organizations often use a **Change Advisory Board (CAB)** to review and approve significant changes.

---

## Common Change Management Requirements

Before implementing changes, organizations typically require:

- Risk analysis
- Testing
- Approval workflows
- Backout planning
- Communication planning

---

## Change Management Lifecycle

### 1. Preparation

Evaluate the current environment and identify business needs.

---

### 2. Planning and Vision

Define:

- Target state
- Scope
- Success criteria

---

### 3. Implementation

Execute the change.

Examples:

- User training
- System configuration
- Deployment activities

---

### 4. Verification

Validate the results through:

- Testing
- Monitoring
- Stakeholder feedback

---

### 5. Documentation

Document:

- What changed
- Why it changed
- When it changed
- Who approved it

Lessons learned should also be recorded.

---

## Technical Change Considerations

Organizations should evaluate technical impacts such as:

### Access Control Changes

Examples:

- Allow lists
- Deny lists
- Firewall rules

---

### Downtime Risk

Changes should be scheduled during approved maintenance windows.

---

### Service Restart Requirements

Some updates may require:

- Service restarts
- System reboots

---

### Legacy Systems

Older systems may create compatibility issues.

---

### Dependencies

Organizations should evaluate how connected systems may be affected.

---

## Change Documentation

Change records often include:

- Updated network diagrams
- Revised procedures
- Change requests
- Ticket updates
- Implementation notes

---

## Continuous Improvement

After each change, organizations should review:

- Success metrics
- Unexpected issues
- Security improvements
- Process optimizations

---

## Common Terms

| Term | Meaning |
|------|---------|
| **CAB** | Change Advisory Board |
| **SOP** | Standard Operating Procedure |
| **CE** | Cryptographic Erase |
| **MDM** | Mobile Device Management |
| **Change Owner** | Person or team initiating the change |

---

## Important Exam Concepts

### Asset Management

Inventory → Tracking → Protection → Secure Disposal

### Change Management

Plan → Approve → Implement → Test → Document

---

## Summary

Asset management focuses on:

- Visibility
- Ownership
- Protection
- Lifecycle control

Change management focuses on:

- Controlled implementation
- Risk reduction
- Operational stability
- Documentation

Together, they support security, accountability, and business continuity.
