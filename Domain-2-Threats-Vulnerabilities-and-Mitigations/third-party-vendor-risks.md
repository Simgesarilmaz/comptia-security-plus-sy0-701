# Third-Party Vendor Risks

Third-party vendor risks refer to security and operational risks introduced by external service providers such as vendors, suppliers, and managed service providers (MSPs).

Potential impacts include:

- Data confidentiality breaches
- Integrity compromise
- Business continuity disruptions

---

## Threat Vectors and Attack Surfaces

### Threat Vector

A threat vector is the method or pathway an attacker uses to gain unauthorized access to a system.

Examples:

- Phishing emails
- USB devices
- Supply chain compromise

### Attack Surface

An attack surface includes all possible points where an attacker may attempt unauthorized access.

Examples:

- APIs
- Open ports
- User accounts
- Cloud services

---

## Vulnerability Types

| Type | Description |
|------|-------------|
| **Hardware** | Security weaknesses in physical components or devices |
| **Software** | Outdated, vulnerable, or backdoored software |
| **Operational** | Weak security policies, poor procedures, or human error |

---

## Vendor Assessment

Before establishing a partnership, organizations should evaluate vendor security practices.

### Common Assessment Methods

- **Security Evaluation**  
  Assess the vendor's overall security posture before engagement

- **Penetration Testing**  
  Simulate attacks to identify weaknesses in vendor systems

- **Audit Rights**  
  Ensure contractual rights to perform security audits

- **Evidence Collection**  
  Gather compliance reports, certifications, and security documentation

---

## Vendor Selection and Monitoring

### Due Diligence

Perform background checks and evaluate:

- Security history
- Regulatory compliance
- Financial stability
- Incident response capabilities

### Continuous Monitoring

Monitor vendors regularly for:

- Performance issues
- Security incidents
- Compliance changes

### Security Questionnaires

Use structured questionnaires to assess:

- Security controls
- Governance processes
- Compliance maturity

## Contracts and Agreements

| Agreement | Purpose |
|-----------|---------|
| **SLA (Service Level Agreement)** | Defines service performance expectations and uptime requirements |
| **NDA (Non-Disclosure Agreement)** | Protects confidential and sensitive information |
| **MSA (Master Service Agreement)** | Defines the general terms and conditions of the business relationship |
| **SOW (Statement of Work)** | Defines project-specific deliverables, timelines, and responsibilities |
| **MOU (Memorandum of Understanding)** | Documents mutual intentions between parties |
| **MOA (Memorandum of Agreement)** | Defines formal responsibilities and commitments |

## Supply Chain Risks

Third-party relationships may introduce risks in multiple areas.

### Hardware Risks

- Counterfeit components
- Tampered hardware
- Embedded malicious chips or firmware

### Software Risks

- Unlicensed software
- Vulnerable dependencies
- Malicious code insertion

### Service Provider Risks

Examples:

- MSPs
- Cloud providers
- SaaS providers

Potential risks include:

- Data exposure
- Misconfigurations
- Loss of service availability

---

## Supply Chain Attacks

A supply chain attack occurs when attackers compromise a weaker third party in order to gain access to the primary target.

### Common Mitigations

- Perform vendor due diligence
- Conduct regular security audits
- Include security requirements in contracts
- Continuously monitor vendor environments

---

## CHIPS Act (2022)

The **CHIPS and Science Act of 2022** is a United States law designed to strengthen supply chain security by increasing domestic semiconductor production and reducing dependency on foreign manufacturing.

---

## Security Safeguards

Organizations should implement the following safeguards:

- Vendor due diligence
- Continuous security monitoring
- Industry and sector collaboration
- Contractual security requirements
- Periodic security assessments
- Incident response coordination
