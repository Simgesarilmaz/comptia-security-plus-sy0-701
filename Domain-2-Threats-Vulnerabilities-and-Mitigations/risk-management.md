# Risk Management

Risk Management is a fundamental process that includes **risk identification, analysis, treatment, monitoring, and reporting**.

---

## Risk Management Lifecycle

### 1. Risk Identification

Risk identification is the proactive process of discovering potential risks.

**Goal:**  
Identify all events that could prevent the organization from achieving its objectives.

**Techniques:**

- Brainstorming
- Checklists
- Interviews
- Scenario Analysis

#### Business Impact Analysis (BIA)

Business Impact Analysis (BIA) determines how critical business functions would be affected during disruptions.

#### Key Metrics

| Metric | Description |
|--------|-------------|
| **RTO (Recovery Time Objective)** | The maximum acceptable time required to restore a business process or system. |
| **RPO (Recovery Point Objective)** | The maximum acceptable amount of data loss measured in time. |
| **MTTR (Mean Time to Repair)** | The average time required to repair and restore a failed system. |
| **MTBF (Mean Time Between Failures)** | The average time between system failures, used as a reliability metric. |

---

### 2. Risk Analysis

Risk analysis evaluates both **likelihood** and **impact**.

Two common analysis methods are used:

| Type | Description |
|------|-------------|
| **Qualitative Analysis** | Evaluates risk using categories such as low, medium, or high. |
| **Quantitative Analysis** | Uses numerical values and financial calculations to assess risk. |

#### Quantitative Components

- **EF (Exposure Factor):** The percentage of asset loss if a threat occurs (0–100%)
- **SLE (Single Loss Expectancy):** Expected financial loss from a single incident  
  Formula: `Asset Value × EF`
- **ARO (Annualized Rate of Occurrence):** Estimated number of times a threat may occur annually
- **ALE (Annualized Loss Expectancy):** Estimated yearly financial loss  
  Formula: `SLE × ARO`

---

### 3. Risk Treatment

Risk treatment involves selecting strategies to manage identified risks.

**Goal:**  
Reduce risk to an acceptable level.

#### Risk Treatment Strategies

- **Avoidance:** Eliminate the activity causing the risk
- **Reduction (Mitigation):** Reduce the likelihood or impact of the risk
- **Transference:** Transfer the risk to another party (such as insurance or contracts)
- **Acceptance:** Accept the risk when the cost of mitigation exceeds the potential loss

#### Additional Concepts

- **Exception:** Temporary approval to deviate from a policy or control
- **Exemption:** Permanent approval to operate outside a policy or control

In both cases, the organization accepts the associated risk.

---

### 4. Risk Monitoring

Risk monitoring involves continuously tracking identified risks and evaluating the effectiveness of implemented controls.

#### Types of Risks Monitored

- **Residual Risk:** The remaining risk after security controls have been applied
- **Control Risk:** The risk that a security control may fail or lose effectiveness over time

---

### 5. Risk Reporting

Risk reporting is the process of communicating risk information to stakeholders.

**Goal:**  
Support accountability and informed decision-making.

---

## Risk Assessment Frequency

Defines how often risk assessments should be performed.

| Type | Description |
|------|-------------|
| **Ad-hoc** | Performed as needed in response to specific events or situations |
| **Recurring** | Performed at regular intervals such as monthly, quarterly, or annually |
| **One-time** | Performed once for a specific project or initiative |
| **Continuous** | Ongoing, real-time monitoring using automation and analytics |

---

## Risk Register

A Risk Register is a documented list of identified risks, their impact, likelihood, and response actions.

### Key Components

| Field | Description |
|--------|-------------|
| **Risk Description** | A clear description of the identified risk |
| **Risk Impact** | The potential business impact (Low / Medium / High) |
| **Risk Likelihood** | The probability of occurrence |
| **Risk Level** | The calculated result based on impact and likelihood |
| **Cost** | The financial impact of the risk or mitigation effort |

---

## Risk Tolerance and Risk Appetite

- **Risk Tolerance:** The maximum level of risk an organization is willing to accept
- **Risk Appetite:** The amount of risk an organization is willing to take to achieve its objectives

### Common Risk Appetite Types

- **Expansionary**
- **Conservative**
- **Neutral**

---

## Key Risk Indicators (KRIs)

Key Risk Indicators (KRIs) are measurable indicators used to predict increasing risk levels.

They help organizations:

- Detect risks early
- Support proactive decision-making
- Align risk monitoring with business objectives

---

## Risk Owner

A Risk Owner is the individual responsible for managing a specific risk.

Responsibilities include:

- Updating the Risk Register
- Implementing mitigation actions
- Monitoring risk status

---

## Summary

| Phase | Description |
|-------|-------------|
| **Identification** | Discovering and documenting risks |
| **Analysis** | Evaluating likelihood and impact |
| **Treatment** | Selecting an appropriate response strategy |
| **Monitoring** | Continuously tracking risks and controls |
| **Reporting** | Communicating risk status and performance |

---

**Note:**  
Risk management is a continuous and dynamic process. Regular assessments, effective communication, and automation are critical for maintaining its effectiveness.
